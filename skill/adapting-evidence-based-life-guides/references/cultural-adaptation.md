# Cultural adaptation

Language and culture are different axes. A guide can be flawlessly grammatical in the target
language and still be unusable because it argues from the source country's assumptions.

## The four things that change

### 1. Register

Match the register a serious local publication would use for the same content — not the register
of the source country, and not a caricature of the target country.

- Keep localisms that are **the actual name of a thing**: agencies, forms, benefits, colloquial
  names citizens really use for a public scheme. Translating these is worse than leaving them:
  readers cannot search for the translated form.
- Strip localisms used as **decoration** — greetings, intensifiers, slang in an otherwise formal
  text. They date fast and read as condescension in an advice corpus.
- No exclamation marks. No moralizing. No "you should have". State cost, state benefit, stop.

### 2. Institutional assumptions

The source guide assumes the reader's world has certain mechanisms. Check each one against the
target country and record the verdict in the substitution table:

| Assumption to test | Why it inverts |
|---|---|
| Mandatory social insurance vs. private accounts | Changes every money item: who pays, when, how much |
| Employer-provided insurance vs. state system | Changes the advice from "notify HR" to "go to the office" |
| Judicial vs. administrative remedies | Changes whether recourse costs a lawyer or a form |
| Conscription / military service | May not exist, or exist as a lottery |
| Registered residence / household permits | Where absent, delete every dependent item |
| Public housing fund / mandatory saving schemes | Where absent, replace with ordinary credit or subsidy |
| Statutory severance vs. at-will employment | Reverses what "your employer owes you" means |
| Universal healthcare vs. tiered contribution | Changes the "where to go when sick" chapter completely |

### 3. Money scales

Never port a foreign amount. Rebuild the cost column from local prices: a procedure, a monitor, a
helmet, a month of a drug, a fine. Then check the *relationship* holds — an item that was "free"
in the source may be expensive locally, which changes its rank but not its validity. Money
figures carry their year, always.

### 4. Cultural sensitivity of the topic itself

Some chapters are culture-dependent in content, not just wording:

- **Marriage and family** — property regimes, prenuptial arrangements, divorce grounds, adoption
  rules, obligations toward parents. Rebuild from the local civil code; do not adapt the framing.
- **Advice toward strangers** — the source's calculus (will stopping to help a stranger pay off?)
  depends on local law on duty to rescue, on Good Samaritan protection, and on liability. Get the
  local legal position, then write the reasoning.
- **Help-seeking behavior** — which authority a citizen trusts with a complaint differs. Point at
  the body that actually has jurisdiction locally.
- **Anything touching religion, honor, or family reputation** — the source's practical advice may
  be sound but unreadable locally. Choose the framing the topic needs in the target country.

## Tone rules that survive adaptation

- Imperative for reader actions, impersonal for information.
- Show both sides when the evidence is mixed; the guide's credibility is its refusal to oversell.
- Prefer the concrete over the abstract: "30 calendar days", not "promptly".
- Never moralize about the reader's choices — the corpus's whole premise is that the reader
  decides once they see costs and benefits.
- State what the item does *not* cover, in `Notas`. Readers trust a guide that names its limits.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Machine translation plus light editing | Preserves foreign laws, amounts, phone numbers and framing |
| Over-localizing | Slang and forced idiom age badly and undermine authority |
| Dropping local terminology to sound neutral | Readers lose the search term they'd use in real life |
| Assuming the target country is "the same but different money" | The institutional layer is the bulk of the work |
| Copying the source's chapter ordering uncritically | Value ranking is local; a chapter may deserve to move up or down |
